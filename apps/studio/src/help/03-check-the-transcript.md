The transcript is the source for everything else — the blog post, the
captions, the clips, the cover. Time spent here is repaid five times over.

## Making it

Open the **Transcript** room and press the transcribe button. It runs on the
worker: a fifty-minute sermon takes about seven minutes. You can leave the
page; the job keeps going and the room shows you where it has got to.

While it transcribes, it also **listens to the delivery** — where the
preacher got loud, where the pace lifted, where the pauses fell. That
measurement is what the Clip Lab later uses to tell a line that lands from a
line that merely reads well.

## Reading it

Read it. Not word by word — but scan it, because two things go wrong often
enough to be worth catching:

- **Names.** Places, people and books of the Bible are where automatic transcription is weakest, and a misspelt name goes straight into the blog post.
- **The opening.** If the first paragraph is announcements, your trim was late. Fix the trim and transcribe again.

Press **Edit** to correct anything. You are editing the text every other
piece will be written from, so a fix here fixes it everywhere.

## Approving it

**Approve** is a person saying they have read this and it is right. It is
not a formality — nothing else in Amplify can know whether the words are
accurate, and the Publish page will warn you if you try to send work out
that came from an unapproved transcript.

> Approve after you have read it, not before you generate the writing. The
> writing is quick to run again; a blog post published with the wrong name
> in it is not quick to take back.

## Doing it again

**Transcribe it again** replaces the transcript. Use it when:

- you re-trimmed the sermon,
- the audio was wrong and you have re-uploaded,
- or the sermon was transcribed before the delivery was being measured, and you want the Clip Lab to have it.

The new transcript arrives unapproved, because nobody has read it yet.
