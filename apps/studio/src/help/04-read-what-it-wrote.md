The **Writing** room holds the words. Four pieces, each written from the
transcript, each on its own page so its buttons sit beside the thing they
act on rather than beside three pieces they have nothing to do with.

## The four pieces

- **Blog post** — the long version for the church website. The sermon as something readable.
- **YouTube packaging** — the title, the description and the tags the full video goes up with.
- **Facebook post** — the short written version, with a link back to the blog post.
- **Metadata** — the sermon's theme, its summary, its scripture references and tags. Less something you read than something the other pieces read: it is what keeps the cover, the captions and the title all pointing at the same sermon.

Open a piece to read it. **Write it again** re-runs that one piece on the
current prompts; **Edit** lets you change the text by hand.

## How to read it

Read it as an editor, not a proofreader. The machine is reliable about what
was said and unreliable about what mattered. So:

- **Check the claim.** If the post says the sermon was about forgiveness and it was about repentance, that is worth fixing and no amount of tidying the prose will fix it.
- **Check the names.** They came from the transcript. If they are wrong there, they are wrong here.
- **Check the opening line.** It is the only sentence most people read.

Anything you edit by hand stays edited. Re-running a piece replaces it, so
edit after you are done regenerating, not before.

## When it all needs doing again

If you have changed the prompts — the wording that tells Amplify how your
church sounds — the writing already on the page was made under the old
wording. **Write it all again on the current prompts**, in the Source room,
re-runs every piece so they match.

> Changing the voice in Settings does not reach back and rewrite what is
> already there. Nothing does, until you ask for it. That is deliberate: a
> setting nobody remembers changing should not quietly rewrite a post that
> has already been read and approved.

## Where the voice comes from

Settings holds your church's voice and its prompts. If the writing sounds
wrong in a way that is the same on every sermon — too formal, too casual,
reaching for words nobody at your church says — that is a prompt to change,
not a piece to edit. Fix it once there and every sermon after it improves.
