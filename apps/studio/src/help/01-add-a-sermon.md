Amplify turns one sermon recording into a week of content: a blog post for
the website, packaging for YouTube, a short written version for Facebook, a
cover picture, and a handful of vertical clips for reels. You bring the
recording and the judgement. It does the reading and the typing.

The work moves left to right along the rail at the top of every sermon —
**Source, Transcript, Writing, Visuals, Clips & reels, Publish** — and each
room is a screenful with one job. You can move between them freely; the rail
tells you what each one is waiting on.

## Adding one

1. On the sermon library, press **Add a sermon**.
2. Give it a **title**. This is what you will look for in six months, so a real title beats a filename.
3. Choose **who preached it**. If they are not on the list, pick *Someone else…* and type their name — they get added to the roster, so next time they are one tap away.
4. Set the **date preached**, and the **service time** if the church has more than one service that day.
5. Press **Add sermon**.

## The two names

Choosing a speaker from the list carries something free text cannot: what
the church actually calls them.

- **Their name** is the name on the record — *Chris Tidwell*. It is what sorts the library and what goes in the credits.
- **What we call them** is the familiar one — *Pastor Chris*. This is the name that reaches the writing, so the blog post sounds like your church rather than a press release.

> If you type a guest's name into the box and leave the second field blank,
> the writing will call them by their full name every time. It takes five
> seconds to fill in and it is the difference between "Chris Tidwell said"
> and "Pastor Chris said" in every sentence of every piece.

## What happens next

Adding a sermon creates the shell and nothing else. Nothing is written, and
no time is spent, until you give it a recording — which is the next chapter.
