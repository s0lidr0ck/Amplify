A fifty-minute sermon usually holds three or four moments that work on their
own in a feed. The **Clips & reels** room finds the candidates so a person
does not have to watch the whole thing again. Choosing between them is still
your job — and the room is built so you can disagree with it quickly.

## Finding them

Press **Find the moments**. It reads the sermon twice: once to gather every
window that could stand alone, and once to rank them against each other. It
takes a minute or two and comes back with a gallery.

Each tile shows the moment itself — a frame seeked out of the sermon — with:

- the **hook**, the opening line, which is the clip as far as a viewer is concerned,
- the **score** on the picture, out of 100,
- the **length**, and which feed it suits,
- and four judgements: **hook, cadence, standalone, emotion**.

## What the four numbers mean

The big score says which clip is strongest. The four say what each one is
*good at*, which is what actually decides between two clips a point apart.

- **Hook** — does the first five seconds stop a thumb.
- **Cadence** — does the delivery have a shape you can hear. Measured off the audio, not guessed from the words.
- **Standalone** — does it make sense with no sermon around it.
- **Emotion** — does it carry weight.

A clip with a low **standalone** score needs the sentence before it to make
sense, and will confuse anybody who meets it cold in a feed. A low
**cadence** with a high hook is a great line delivered flat.

## Looking at one

Click a tile and you get everything about that moment on one screen: the
sermon scrubbed to it, both trim marks, all four judgements, and the
reasoning behind the score in the ranker's own words.

**Why this moment** is worth reading. It cites the actual measurements, so
it will tell you things like *"cadence reads 30 but that is the transcript,
not the delivery; energy 85 with rising intensity marks it"* — which is a
claim you can check by pressing play.

## Fixing the edges

The marks under the player work the same way as the sermon trim. Nudge the
start back two seconds to catch the run-up, or pull the end in to lose a
trailing "amen". **Save these times** only lights up once you have actually
moved something.

> The first second is the whole clip. If the hook starts mid-word, no amount
> of editing later will fix it — move the mark.

## Cutting it

**Cut it** sends the clip to the worker, which cuts a real file out of the
sermon. When it comes back the tile says **cut**, and there is a video to
download.

**Discard** removes a clip from the gallery. There is no cost to being
ruthless: ten suggestions where three are worth cutting is a normal week,
and discarding the other seven makes the room easier to read.
